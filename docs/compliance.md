# Rule checks for apparel sold in New Zealand and Australia

Checked 29 October 2026. Run `npm run plm -- compliance`. These checks find styles and orders whose records look inconsistent with the rules below. They check what is recorded for the label, not the label itself, and they do not replace a test laboratory or legal advice. Each style's `markets` field (NZ, AU or both) says which market rules apply. The full standards (AS/NZS 2622, AS/NZS 1957, ISO 3758, AS/NZS 1249) are sold by Standards New Zealand and Standards Australia; read them before relying on a check.

| Check | What the data shows | Rule and source | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| nz-fibre-content | A style sold in New Zealand has no fibre content recorded. | Consumer Information Standards (Fibre Content Labelling) Regulations 2000 (SR 2000/154) adopt AS/NZS 2622 with variations: textile products must state fibre content on a permanent label, in English, clearly legible, letters at least 1.5 mm high. Supplying without one is an offence under the Fair Trading Act 1986. [Regulations](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2000/154/en/latest/), [Commerce Commission guidance](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/fair-trading/consumer-information-standards/fibre-content-labelling/) | Some goods (hosiery, baby bibs) may use a removable label instead. The check looks only for a recorded fibre content. |
| fibre-total | A fibre content statement's percentages do not add to 100. Parts separated by a semicolon (shell; lining; fill) are checked separately. | Same regulations and AS/NZS 2622: fibre content is stated as percentages of the whole, or of each named part. | Reads the percentages in the text. It does not check generic fibre names against AS/NZS 2450. |
| nz-care-english | A style sold in New Zealand has no written care instructions. | Consumer Information Standards (Care Labelling) Regulations 2000 (SR 2000/155): care instructions on a permanent label, legible and in English. Symbols alone do not meet the New Zealand rule. [Regulations](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2000/155/en/latest/), [Commerce Commission guidance](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/fair-trading/consumer-information-standards/care-labelling/) | Checks that instructions are recorded, not that they are adequate for the fabric. |
| au-care-label | A style sold in Australia has neither care instructions nor care symbols recorded. | Consumer Goods (Care Labelling) Information Standard 2023 (F2023L01187), under the Australian Consumer Law: clothing and textiles carry care instructions on a permanent, legible label, following AS/NZS 1957 (as varied) or ISO 3758. From 5 March 2024 symbols alone, words alone or both are allowed. [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/search-mandatory-standards/care-labelling-for-clothing-and-textiles-mandatory-standard), [instrument](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L01187/asmade) | Some made-up goods may use a removable ticket where a permanent label is impractical. |
| nightwear-category | A style marked as children's nightwear has no fire hazard category (1 to 4). | Australia: Consumer Goods (Children's Nightwear and Limited Daywear and Paper Patterns for Children's Nightwear) Safety Standard 2017, based on AS/NZS 1249:2014, covering sizes 00 to 14. New Zealand: Product Safety Standards (Children's Nightwear and Limited Daywear Having Reduced Fire Hazard) Regulations 2016 adopt AS/NZS 1249:2014. [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/search-mandatory-standards/nightwear-for-children-mandatory-standard), [NZ regulations](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2016/58/en/latest/), [Commerce Commission](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/fair-trading/product-safety-standards/childrens-nightwear) | The category comes from the garment's design, fabric and burn test, by a specialist laboratory. This system records it; it does not decide it. |
| nightwear-label | The recorded fire label does not match the category. | Same standards: categories 1 to 3 carry the white low fire hazard label ("CAUTION: Keep away from heat and flame"), category 4 the red high fire danger label ("WARNING: High fire danger. Keep away from heat and flame"). | Checks the recorded label type only. |
| nightwear-test | A children's nightwear style has no burn test report reference on file. | Operator policy in this base: keep the laboratory report that supports the category with the style. | Change the policy in a migration if your testing is held elsewhere. |
| factory-audit | An open production order ships after the factory's social audit expires, or the factory has no audit on file. | Operator policy in this base: bulk only from a factory with a current social compliance audit (amfori BSCI, SMETA, WRAP or similar) on the ex-factory date. Large Australian businesses also report on supply chain risk under the Modern Slavery Act 2018 (Cth). | The audit scheme and expiry are what the operator records. The check does not read audit reports. |
| pp-before-bulk | A production order is in production or shipped with no approved pre-production sample for the style. | Operator policy in this base, matching common buying terms: bulk starts after the pre-production sample is approved. | Change it in a migration if your terms differ. |

Also flagged by `/attention` (operator policy, not law): factory audits expiring within `audit_warning_days` (60 by default, in `plm.settings`) and materials whose season demand is below the supplier's minimum order.

To clear a finding, fix the record: write the fibre content and care text as they will appear on the label, record the category, label and test report once the laboratory reports, chase the audit date, or hold the order. Never edit the rule to make a finding disappear. If a rule here is out of date, update this file and the check in a new migration together, with the date and source.

Not checked here: country of origin marking, product safety for drawstrings, small parts or button batteries, chemical restrictions, customs tariff classification and duty rates, and claims such as "organic" or "recycled". Those need their own evidence.
